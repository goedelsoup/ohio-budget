# crates

Rust crates implementing the domain computer — the retrieval, calculation, and feature
engineering capabilities that agents use to work with the corpus without loading it
wholesale into context.

See [crates conventions](../.yidam/.vendor/prelude/guidelines/directories.md#crates) for
the three capability types (connectors, calculators, feature engineering) and the index
layer.

The domain computer is built incrementally. Ohio budget data is published by several
distinct authorities in several distinct shapes — the Legislative Service Commission
issues per-agency analyses and comparison documents around each bill stage, the Office of
Budget and Management publishes monthly financial reports and the state's audited annual
financial report, and the Treasurer publishes transaction-level detail. None of them share
an identifier scheme, which is why reconciliation is a calculator concern rather than a
connector concern.

Two computations are central to inquiry and neither is available from any upstream source:
the **gap** between appropriated authority and actual disbursement for a line item in a
period, and **lineage resolution** — deciding whether a line item in one biennium is the
successor of a differently-coded line item in an earlier one. Both are pure functions over
corpus data and belong in calculators.

Specific connectors and calculators are proposed and approved in
[`.yidam/decisions/proposals.yml`](../.yidam/decisions/proposals.yml) rather than named
here speculatively.

A semantic index over the corpus is not planned from genesis. At forty nodes the corpus
fits comfortably in context; the index earns its cost when the corpus grows past the point
where an agent can read it whole, which extraction phases across sixteen fiscal years will
reach quickly.
